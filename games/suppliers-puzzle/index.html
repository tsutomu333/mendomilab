The Supplier's Puzzle: a heijunka simulation game

Toyota needs 8,000 cars this month and you run two suppliers. Change the build order, the people and the machines, and find the cheapest way for the supplier — not just for Toyota. An animated one-month simulation shows where the cost really comes from.

What participants learn

  • Why a customer's build order decides how much capacity and stock its suppliers must carry.
  • Heijunka: leveling both the volume and the mix of models over time.
  • Why large batches force suppliers to choose between capacity for the peak and stock built ahead — and why both cost money.
  • How suppliers should size people and machines when the order changes.
  • Why lowering supplier cost is a shared job, not just a price demand.

Groups of three work well: one plays Toyota (the build order), one plays Supplier A and one plays Supplier B.

How a session runs

  1. Run the month as it starts. Toyota's line never stops. Nothing seems wrong.
  2. Debrief. The facilitator asks: "Who chose this cost — the supplier or Toyota?"
  3. Suppliers cut cost alone. They remove people and machines and watch the warehouse fill up.
  4. Toyota changes the build order. Suppliers resize their people and machines each time and compare the cost.
  5. Back to your supply chain. Where do uneven orders reach your own suppliers or your own floor?

Questions

What is heijunka?

Heijunka means leveling production: both the volume and the mix of products are spread evenly over time, instead of building one model in a long run and then switching. It is one of the foundations of the Toyota Production System. See heijunka examples with numbers and mura, muri, muda examples.

Why does the customer's order matter to the supplier?

A supplier has to be ready for the busiest day its customer creates. With large batches, that peak forces the supplier to carry either idle capacity or stock built ahead. The game lets players see that cost move.

Do I need to be a lean expert to run it?

No. The Pro facilitator guide gives you the session plan, the questions to ask and the answers people usually give.

Does it need installation or sign-up?

No. The free version runs in a browser on itch.io. The Pro version is a single file that runs offline in any modern browser.
