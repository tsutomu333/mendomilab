Lean vs Toyota Production System: what is the difference?

The Toyota Production System (TPS) is the way Toyota built up in its own plants over decades. Lean is the name researchers in the United States gave to it when they studied it and described it so other companies could use it. Most of the content is the same. The differences are in where each came from and what each tends to emphasize.

The difference at a glance

Toyota Production System (TPS)Lean
OriginToyota's plants (Taiichi Ohno and colleagues)US research (the MIT study of the world auto industry)
When the name appearedTook shape from the post-war years to the 1970s"Lean production" was used in a 1988 paper and popularized by the 1990 book The Machine That Changed the World
PillarsJust-in-time and jidoka (automation with a human touch)Five principles: value, value stream, flow, pull, perfection (Lean Thinking, 1996)
EmphasisMaking problems visible on the floor and developing people who keep improvingRemoving waste and creating a flow of value for the customer
Where it is usedMainly Toyota, its group companies and suppliersManufacturing, and also hospitals, software, offices and services

What they share

  • Reducing waste such as overproduction, inventory and waiting (see the 7 wastes)
  • Pull: an earlier process makes only what the next process used
  • Leveling volume and mix (heijunka)
  • Producing at the customer's pace (takt time)

Where they tend to differ

1. Tools first or thinking first
When lean is introduced, it often starts with tools: kanban, 5S, visual management. In TPS the tools are means to make problems visible so people can fix them. Copying the tools without people who see and solve problems rarely lasts.

2. Jidoka
The second pillar of TPS, jidoka, means stopping a machine or a line when something is abnormal so defects do not flow downstream. Lean summaries sometimes give it less weight than just-in-time and waste removal.

3. Developing people
TPS cannot be separated from developing people who keep improving. Working with people who spent long careers on Toyota's shop floors, I came to see that much of Toyota's strength comes from what they call mendomi: leaders and seniors who genuinely look after and develop the people around them.

Which word should you use?

Either is fine. What matters is agreeing on the goal. If the only goal is "less inventory", you may get stock-outs and overtime. Make the purpose clear first, producing what the customer needs when they need it, and then choose the tools.

Try it in a game

  • Overproduction Factory: follow "never stop, keep building", ship every unit, and still lose money. A hands-on way to feel just-in-time.
  • The Supplier's Puzzle: change the build order and watch the supplier's costs change. A hands-on way to feel heijunka.

Questions

Is lean the same as the Toyota Production System?

Mostly, yes. TPS is the system Toyota developed. Lean is the name US researchers gave it when they studied TPS and described it for other companies.

Who coined the term lean production?

It was used in a 1988 paper from the MIT auto industry research program and popularized by the 1990 book The Machine That Changed the World.

What are the two pillars of TPS?

Just-in-time and jidoka. Jidoka means stopping when something is abnormal so defects do not flow to the next process.

Why do lean programs fail?

Often because tools are copied without developing people who see and solve problems. Agree on the purpose first and develop people who keep improving.
