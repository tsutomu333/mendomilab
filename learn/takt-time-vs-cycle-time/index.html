Takt time vs cycle time: formula, examples and staffing

Takt time is the pace the customer requires — how many seconds you have per unit. Cycle time is how long a process or operator actually takes to complete one unit. Takt time is the target; cycle time is your current speed. Comparing the two helps you check whether you can meet demand, whether staffing fits, and whether you are overproducing.

Takt time formula

Takt time = available working time per day ÷ units required per day

Available time excludes breaks, meetings and other planned stops.

ExampleCalculationTakt time
480 min available, 480 units a day28,800 s ÷ 48060 s
450 min available, 300 units a day27,000 s ÷ 30090 s
20 days × 480 min available (after breaks), 9,600 units a month480 a day → 28,800 s ÷ 48060 s

When demand or available time changes, takt time changes. Machine speed and headcount are not part of the formula.

What cycle time is

Cycle time is the interval at which a process releases one unit. For a single operator, it is the time to complete their work sequence once. Measure it several times with a stopwatch and look at the variation, not just the average.

What the comparison tells you

SituationWhat happensWhat to do
Cycle time < takt timeYou can build faster than needed. Produce beyond the quantity needed and you overproduce; stop at the quantity needed and there is waiting timeBuild to takt. Use the spare capacity by rebalancing people and improving
Cycle time > takt timeThe process risks missing demand: overtime or shortagesFind the cause at the bottleneck; improve the work or rebalance people and equipment
Cycle time ≈ takt timeWork flows at the customer's paceReduce variation

How many people you need

People needed (estimate) = total manual work time per unit ÷ takt time

If the work across four processes adds up to 240 seconds and takt time is 60 seconds, the calculation gives 240 ÷ 60 = 4 people — provided the work can be split so each person stays within 60 seconds. If it adds up to 250 seconds, that is 4.17. Before adding a fifth person, look for waste and try to bring the total within 240 seconds; if you cannot, consider a fifth person or a change in working time.

Takt time vs cycle time vs lead time

Lead time is the time from receiving an order (or material) until the customer receives the product. Takt and cycle time are about pace per unit; lead time is about how long the customer waits overall. The more inventory sits between processes, the longer the lead time.

Common mistakes

  • Calling machine speed "takt time." That is cycle time. Takt time comes from customer demand.
  • Including breaks in available time. Nobody produces during breaks. Including them makes takt look longer than it is, and you fall behind.
  • "If we can go faster, we should." Producing beyond what is needed is overproduction. Build to takt and use the extra capacity by freeing people for other work or improvement.

Questions

What is the difference between takt time and cycle time?

Takt time is the pace the customer requires (available time ÷ demand); you calculate it. Cycle time is how long a process actually takes to complete one unit; you measure it.

What is the takt time formula?

Takt time = available working time ÷ units required. With 480 minutes available and 480 units a day, takt time is 28,800 s ÷ 480 = 60 seconds.

What happens if cycle time is shorter than takt time?

Produce beyond the quantity needed and you overproduce; stop at the quantity needed and there is waiting time. Build to takt and use the spare capacity by rebalancing people and improving.

How do you calculate the number of operators needed?

Divide the total manual work time per unit by takt time for an estimate. 240 seconds of work at a 60-second takt gives 4 people, if the work can be split so each person stays within takt.
