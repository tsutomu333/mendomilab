About Mendomi Lab

What mendomi means

Mendomi (面倒見) is a Japanese word for looking after people. In the workplace it means caring about a colleague at work and beyond, until trust grows to the point where people think, "For this boss, for this teammate, I'll go the extra mile."

Praising, correcting and watching over are part of how that trust is built. They are not a communication technique to use on people; they are how a relationship is formed.

Why a lab of games?

Lean tools are easy to copy and hard to make stick. In my experience the difference is not the tool but the relationship between the people using it. So the games here do two jobs:

  • They let people feel a lean idea — overproduction, flow, leveled production — instead of only hearing about it.
  • They give a leader or trainer a reason to talk: to ask questions, to praise good thinking, to correct, and to watch people try again.

Improvement should never mean squeezing people or suppliers. When a game shows that fewer people are needed, the point is what to do with the capacity you just freed — not who to let go.

Who makes the games

I'm Tsutomu Takagi. I worked as an improvement consultant at OJT Solutions, a Toyota group company that develops people who can lead Toyota-style improvement. There I worked alongside hundreds of former Toyota shop-floor supervisors, and I came to feel that the real source of Toyota's strength was mendomi.

Mendomi Lab is my independent project. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by or sponsored by Toyota Motor Corporation or OJT Solutions. The games and guides are my own work and do not use any company's training materials or client information.

Our promise in every game

  1. It helps people build trust — between leaders and members, and between colleagues.
  2. It leaves room to praise, to correct and to watch over, instead of just giving the answer.
  3. It points back to the participants' own workplace, so the conversation continues after the session.
